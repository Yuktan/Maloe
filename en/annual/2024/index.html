2024 Annual Review

This year felt less like a calendar and more like a series of flights. I kept moving, and slowly learned to look inward too.

Contents

01

Opening

It’s December 12, and I finally have a quiet moment. So here’s a slightly stream-of-consciousness year-end recap.

I thought I’d quickly go through the year month by month, but that isn’t how I remember it. My memories are divided up by flights instead. After I posted my early 2023 review last November, work and all kinds of headaches came rushing in. It felt like everything had gone back to normal, even as things were quietly changing.

The first half of the year was all about progress. Things moved fast around the middle of the year, and I got the result I had been working toward. I should have been happy, though looking back, I’m not so sure it was entirely a good thing. On my birthday, while hiking in Alishan, I told myself it was time to look inward.

In the second half, I had too much headspace. Without noticing, I kept staring at the parts of my life I hadn’t figured out. People say longing for the other side feels like the mood of our time. Suddenly I was approaching thirty, and every night after work a giant question mark appeared in my head. Advice from older people, friends going through the same uncertainty, even younger people sharing their worries—bit by bit, it all made me more anxious.

Years ago, I said I was like a curled-up leaf slowly opening. Did I curl back up this year?

02

1. Getting a feel for the stock market

Only a beginner would feel bold enough to talk about the stock market, so here I am. I’m not trying to brag about returns. Trading—and the other people doing it—has taught me quite a bit about life:

  1. A share price reflects the market’s confidence. What you earn comes from the gap between your judgment and everyone else’s.
  2. Knowing when to cut a loss or take a gain takes patience—and a handle on your own greed. I don’t buy the “it isn’t a loss if I don’t sell” logic.
  3. If you don’t have the time or energy, don’t invest blindly. Any money you make while asleep depends on the work you did while awake. When you miss a rise, maybe you missed a key piece of news—or didn’t connect what you knew to a decision.

As of today, my money-weighted return for the year is xx%+. Fingers crossed it holds.

03

2. Learning to do a handstand

A lot of friends know I’ve been working on handstands and ask why I’m so set on them. In yoga, turning upside down can mean changing the way you see things—looking from another angle, or turning your attention inward instead of always searching outside yourself. For me, going against gravity puts me in a kind of perfect meditative state.

Learning a handstand means getting past the fear of falling and finding the courage to lift both feet off the ground. It’s the opposite of hiking, where you always want solid footing. Skateboarding already taught me how afraid I am of falling: as soon as I lose my balance, I panic and try to force my body back under control. The harder I try, the worse it gets.

My Pilates teacher—whom I call “Teacher”—said that when I started, my breathing was as fast as my shaking. In November, I finally held a handstand for more than five minutes. She said, “You’ve learned how to breathe.”

Turns out, being alive doesn’t mean you know how to breathe.

If we’re friends on Instagram, my Story Highlights have the whole handstand journey.

04

3. Making room for loss

Once, while diving for pearls in Animal Crossing, I got a line back: “The thrill of gambling is in losing.” I know this is supposed to be about what I gained this year. But loss means there was something to gain in the first place. The more you have, the more you can lose—and the more you worry about it.

Everything flows. Nothing stays.

05

Closing

And that’s the end of this year’s review. A little sudden, right? Endings often are. I hope the moon keeps shining on the people we hold dear, and that every new year brings my friends peace and good health.
