2021 Annual Review

Flowers, coffee, plants, games, and two trips to Yunnan. Nothing earth-shattering, but I was happy for most of it.

01

Opening

I was happy for most of this year. Looking back, there isn’t a huge list of things to remember, which is pretty much what I expected. It saves space in my already limited brain storage.

02

January: Clubhouse

When I type “Clubhouse,” I don’t really mean the voice-chat app. I mean the conversation that night in a room called “Young People Across the Strait: Everyone Asks Everyone.” The hardest barrier to communication is often the position we start from. If we can’t try to see things from the other person’s side, we won’t get far in solving anything.

That discussion taught me two things. First, work on the actual problem, not someone’s tone—whether they sound angry or sarcastic. Second, don’t let emotion make the decision for you. Some issues can’t be explained, much less solved, in a sentence or two. Things we say in anger can change the way we think and leave marks on other people that are hard to erase, especially when our words are cruel.

03

April: Learning the names of flowers

This was my second spring enjoying the flowers in Beijing. After moving from Yayuncun in Chaoyang to Xierqi in Haidian, I thought I’d lost the luxury of stepping downstairs and straight into a park. But the new neighborhood keeps surprising me. There are flowers in every season, practically all the popular Beijing varieties, and I even ran into a little hedgehog on my way home from a late night at work.

As for telling flowers apart—especially those in the rose family—I still go back to a Zhihu answer by my favorite plant writer, Manmei, on how to distinguish peach, apricot, plum, cherry, pear, and flowering plum blossoms. After that, you just have to go outside in the right season and look for yourself.

04

June: Hockey sticks and hairy backs

Working on overseas markets for a Chinese company means big reorganizations are never far away. During a quieter stretch, I read Strategy Beyond the Hockey Stick. I don’t usually pick up books about strategy, because getting a plan to work in real life is a whole other story. Shrug.

05

September: Coffee in the morning, plants at night

I kept telling myself this year: don’t get all your sense of achievement from work. So I made coffee part of my daily routine instead of just a hobby, using the pour and the feel of it to practice patience. After @eimo’s partner gave my little espresso machine a much-needed clean, it was back in action—thank you, Wei Si! I also found a reliable everyday bean: the honor goes to S.O.E’s Lychee Bubbles.

I also have to mention the plants. All that attentive care paid off: my nandina grew new shoots in the Beijing suburbs, and just yesterday my young monstera put out its first split leaf. Fun fact: those holes help a monstera cope with life on the rainforest floor, where sunlight is scarce.

06

December: Genshin Impact

Friends who know me well know I’m hooked on Genshin Impact. Back when I was a Zelda player, I called it a copy. Then I actually started playing and spent enough money to make a fool of my former self. I joked, “There’s no problem one 648-yuan top-up can’t solve.”

A new character arrived in the latest update, and like a lot of players, I was upset. I’d saved so many 648-yuan top-ups for Xiao, a five-star character, that I didn’t even know where to start complaining. After reading a Zhihu discussion about another player who had spent 400,000 yuan and then quit, I decided to wait before quitting myself. I want to know why I fell for this game in the first place. What am I looking for here, and what am I really paying for?

I’ve always thought that engaging with the world is one way to get to know myself. I still can’t answer @liqi’s big question—“What’s your dream?”—but paying attention to what makes me happy, angry, or sad gets me a little closer.

07

The place I kept going back to: Yunnan

I went to Yunnan twice, once in winter and once in summer.

On a warm winter night, our plane landed as the new year began. We drove through a city full of fireworks, and for a moment I felt the joy of saying goodbye to one year and hello to the next. Some things are like fireworks: beautiful and gone too fast. I have to mention a Bilibili video this year about sacrificing two FPV drones to film the center of a firework burst. The last time fireworks moved me that much was @cbvivi’s video of a spectacular fireworks festival.

In the heat of summer, I walked through a tropical rainforest, fell for the rice noodles with water bamboo shoots in Jianshui, and saw the giant moth from Animal Crossing at the Xishuangbanna botanical garden. I even went back for beef noodles two days in a row because the cook was so welcoming.

It isn’t that Yunnan is so perfect I had to keep returning. Whenever @liqi and I chose where to go, I felt the loss of the freedom we had to travel in 2019. Life with the pandemic was something we couldn’t just wish away.

08

Closing

And that’s pretty much my year-end review.

Thank you to the people I love and to my cats for keeping me company this year. Here’s to an even sweeter next one.
